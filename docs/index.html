AnvilCAD

Free, open-source parametric 3D CAD written in Rust, with its own geometry kernel. If you know SOLIDWORKS, the workflow will feel familiar: a feature tree, sketches on planes or faces, and fully defined sketches.

View on GitHub Roadmap

The source is public on GitHub under MIT OR Apache-2.0. There are no prebuilt downloads yet, so build from source with cargo run --release -p anvil-app. The binary is called anvilcad.

01 / Capabilities

What works today

Every item here is in the code now. The workflow follows SOLIDWORKS conventions: pick a plane or a face, draw a sketch, define it fully, and turn it into a feature in the tree.

  • Sketcher

    Line, centerline, rectangle, circle and centerpoint arc, plus construction geometry. Drag points and circles and the relations stay satisfied.

  • Relations and Smart Dimension

    Relations including tangent, concentric and midpoint. Smart Dimension covers lengths, diameters, radii and angles. Degrees of freedom are reported as you go, and conflicting relations are highlighted.

  • Extrudes

    Extruded Boss and Extruded Cut with Blind, Mid Plane, Through All and Through All - Both end conditions.

  • Revolves and patterns

    Revolved Boss and Revolved Cut, plus Linear Pattern and Circular Pattern.

  • Fillet, Chamfer and Shell

    Fillets and chamfers on straight edges between flat faces, and on circular edges where a flat face meets a cylinder. Shell hollows a part to a uniform wall.

  • Planes and faces

    Sketch on the Front, Top or Right plane, on reference planes you add, or directly on a face of the model.

  • Variables and equations

    Global variables with equations. Type =width into any dimension to link it.

  • Materials and properties

    Assign a material to get mass properties. Custom properties and part templates are supported too.

  • Files and export

    Native .anvprt parts are versioned JSON, written atomically. Import STEP parts (exact solids for plane and cylinder bodies, meshes for everything else). Export to STEP AP214 with exact geometry when the part is exact, plus STL and OBJ. Every change can be undone and redone.

03 / Kernels

Two kernels, one rebuild

Exact geometry where it is supported, a faceted fallback everywhere else.

anvil-brep is an exact boundary-representation kernel. It works with exact lines, circles, ellipses, planes and cylinders, and does exact booleans and exact fillets and chamfers on straight edges. anvil-kernel is a faceted kernel that covers everything else: revolves, shells, fillets on curved edges and imported bodies. A rebuild uses the exact kernel for as long as every feature so far is one it supports, and hands over to the faceted kernel at the first feature it cannot build. A feature is never built worse than it was before.

On the bundled sample part, the extrudes, cuts, linear pattern and first fillet build exactly, and the result matches the faceted-only volume to within 0.014 percent.

04 / Migration

Bring your SOLIDWORKS parts

Your feature tree comes with you, not just the shape.

anvil-migrate converts SOLIDWORKS parts into AnvilCAD parts with their feature tree. It runs on a Windows machine with SOLIDWORKS installed and checks each translated feature by comparing its volume with what SOLIDWORKS reports. When a feature cannot be rebuilt yet, it comes across as an imported body at that point in the tree, and the features after it stay parametric.

05 / Principles

Stability first

A CAD tool that crashes or quietly produces a broken solid costs more than one that is slow. These rules are enforced in the code, not just written down.

  1. Transactional edits

    An edit either applies completely or leaves the part as it was.

  2. Every exact solid is topology-checked

    Solids from the exact kernel are checked before they are used.

  3. Property-based tests on booleans

    Randomized boxes on an integer grid are combined and the results compared with exact volumes.

  4. UI tests on the real interface

    Tests drive the real UI with simulated mouse input, so sketching, dimensioning, dragging, face picking, extruding and undo are covered without opening a window.

06 / Architecture

Eight crates, dependencies point one way

Geometry knows nothing about the UI, and each crate can be tested on its own.

  1. anvil-app

    The desktop application, built on egui and drawn with wgpu: Metal on macOS, Vulkan or GL on Linux, DX12 or Vulkan on Windows.

  2. anvil-migrate

    SOLIDWORKS to AnvilCAD converter. Runs on Windows with SOLIDWORKS installed.

  3. anvil-step

    STEP reader and exact STEP export. Imports bodies as exact solids where it can, and as watertight meshes otherwise.

  4. anvil-io

    Faceted STEP, STL and OBJ export.

  5. anvil-model

    The parametric part: feature tree, rebuild, equations, materials, custom properties and the .anvprt file format.

  6. anvil-sketch

    2D sketcher: entities, relations, the constraint solver and profile extraction.

  7. anvil-kernel

    Faceted kernel for everything the exact kernel does not cover yet.

  8. anvil-brep

    Exact boundary-representation kernel: exact curves and surfaces, booleans, straight-edge fillets and chamfers.

07 / Roadmap

What comes next

Roughly in order. Nothing here has a date attached.

  1. next

    Freeform modelling in the kernel

    Booleans and blends on B-spline faces, surfaces of revolution, and curved-edge fillets built exactly. Planes, cylinders, cones, tori, spheres and B-spline faces already import from STEP as exact geometry.

  2. now

    CAM

    2.5D adaptive clearing built from published research, facing, profiling and drilling; feature recognition; a tool and feeds library; simulation with gouge and collision checks; and community post processors checked by a verifier.

  3. then

    More part features

    A hole feature with standard sizes, mirror, sweep, loft, multi-body parts and sheet metal.

  4. then

    Assemblies and drawings

    Parts positioned together with mates, and 2D drawings made from them.

  5. then

    PDM

    A versioned vault with check-in and check-out, metadata, life-cycle states and an open API.

  6. later

    PLM

    Bills of materials, change management, requirements and quality records, exchanging data through open standards.
